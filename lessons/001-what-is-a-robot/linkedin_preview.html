What makes a machine a robot—and what does not?

Professor OS Class 1 gives learners a durable starting model by separating three ideas that are often conflated:

• Robot identity: the physical machine and the task its actuators perform.
• Decision authority: whether immediate actions come from a human, a stored program, or autonomous software.
• Feedback: whether measurements influence current or future actions.
• Engineering realism: why commanded speed and measured motion are not always the same.

Students apply the ideas to a thermostat, traffic signal, teleoperated rover, industrial arm, and autonomous vacuum. They then calculate RoboRover’s ideal distance and run a Python simulation comparing three decision-authority modes.

Try the lab by changing the command sequences and obstacle measurements before running the code. Ask students to predict the final positions—and explain whether each mode is using feedback.
