A robot cannot navigate reliably by sensing the outside world alone. It also needs to estimate what its wheels have done.

This Professor OS lesson introduces that bridge from measurement to motion:

• Convert encoder ticks into wheel angle and distance.
• Compare left and right wheel travel to estimate straight motion, turning, and rotation in place.
• Use a midpoint-heading update to simulate differential-drive odometry in Python.
• Investigate why slip, unequal wheels, backlash, timing, and calibration errors cause drift.

The lesson combines equations, a worked RoboRover example, executable assertions, a plotted simulation, prediction questions, and a square-path challenge. Students are asked to predict each motion before running the code, making the simulation a test of reasoning rather than a decorative output.

Try the lab, change one wheel’s tick count, and observe how a small asymmetry changes the estimated path.
