A robot does not see a finished map. It builds one gradually from partial, noisy observations.

This lesson introduces the foundations of robotic mapping through RoboRover:

• the difference between a sensor snapshot and a persistent world model;
• occupied, free, and unknown space;
• converting robot-relative measurements into map coordinates;
• why calibration, heading error, sensor noise, and moving objects matter;
• a hands-on Python occupancy-map exercise with prediction checks and assertions.

The central idea is simple but important: unknown is not the same as free. A reliable robot must represent not only what it believes, but also where that belief is uncertain and when it was last checked.

Try the lab, predict the cell counts before running it, then extend the map with a third observation batch. What assumptions would your own mapping system need to make?
