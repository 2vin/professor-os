A shortest path is not always the path with the fewest moves.

In this Professor OS lesson, students use RoboRover to understand how Dijkstra’s algorithm chooses a least-cost route through a weighted graph—and how that idea connects to real robot navigation.

Students learn to:
• distinguish edge count from total route cost;
• trace tentative distances, relaxation, and predecessor updates;
• implement Dijkstra’s algorithm with Python’s heapq priority queue;
• test muddy, faster, and unreachable routes; and
• recognize why map quality and cost design determine whether a plan works in the real world.

The hands-on lab turns a small graph into an experiment: change one floor condition, predict the new route, then verify it in code. Try the lab with your students and ask what the edge costs should represent for time, energy, risk, or a combined objective.
