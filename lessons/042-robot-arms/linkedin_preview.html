A robot arm is more than a collection of motors. Its behavior emerges from the geometry of connected links, the motion allowed by joints, and the independent coordinates we call degrees of freedom.

This Professor OS lesson guides students from a simple two-link planar arm to a working Python simulation. Students learn to:

• distinguish links from joints and actuators
• count independent degrees of freedom
• relate relative joint angles to absolute link directions
• calculate and plot elbow and hand positions
• recognize workspace limits, backlash, payload effects, and singular configurations

The strongest learning moment comes from prediction: students choose joint angles, estimate where the hand will land, run the simulation, and revise their model. That turns forward-kinematics preparation into an experiment rather than a vocabulary exercise.

Try the target-point lab with RoboRover’s 0.30 m and 0.20 m links, then compare two different elbow configurations that reach a similar location. Which would be safer or more useful on a real robot?
