A robot cannot execute “move one metre” as a complete motion command. It needs a time-based plan: where it should be, how fast it should move, and how its acceleration should change.

This Professor OS lesson develops that idea through RoboRover and a quintic trajectory:

• distinguish a geometric path from a time-parameterized trajectory;
• derive position, velocity, and acceleration from normalized time;
• connect shorter motion times to higher speed and acceleration demands;
• verify the mathematics with executable Python assertions;
• search for the shortest duration that satisfies speed and acceleration limits.

The lesson also makes an important engineering distinction: a smooth mathematical reference does not guarantee smooth physical motion. Motor saturation, sampling, calibration, traction, and tracking control still matter.

Try the lab by changing the duration and predicting how the curves will scale before running the code. What is the fastest motion your hypothetical RoboRover could safely execute?
