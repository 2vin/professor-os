A behavior tree can decide that a robot should dock—but it does not need to contain the camera driver, battery monitor, motor controller, or docking implementation.

This lesson introduces the ROS 2 communication model through RoboRover:

• Nodes organize focused software responsibilities.
• Topics carry recurring observations and commands.
• Services handle short, bounded request-and-response operations.
• Actions manage longer tasks with acceptance, feedback, cancellation, and a final result.

The accompanying Python simulation makes the concepts testable without requiring a ROS 2 installation. Students observe fresh versus stale battery data, service unavailability, action-goal acceptance, and the importance of timeouts and failure behavior.

Try the graph-design activity, predict the program’s decisions, then extend the lab with an emergency-state branch or a dashboard subscriber. The goal is not to memorize interface names, but to choose communication patterns that match the timing and failure needs of a real robot.
