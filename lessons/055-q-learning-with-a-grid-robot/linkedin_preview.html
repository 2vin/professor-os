How does a robot learn that one action is better than another when nobody gives it a complete route?

This Professor OS lesson introduces Q-learning through a small grid rover and connects the mathematics to executable Python.

Students learn to:

• represent a robot’s situation as a state and its choices as actions;
• interpret Q values as estimates of discounted future reward;
• calculate a temporal-difference update by hand;
• run an epsilon-greedy learning experiment and inspect the resulting policy;
• question whether a clean simulation is trustworthy on physical hardware.

The lesson deliberately moves from a simple table to real engineering concerns: sensor noise, wheel slip, latency, changing conditions, and safety overrides. Students can also modify the reward structure or add obstacles to observe how design choices shape behavior.

Try the lab, change one assumption, and inspect what the rover learns.
