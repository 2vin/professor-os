Safe autonomy requires more than a planner that produces plausible commands. This lesson guides students from RoboRover’s navigation stack into the engineering questions that must surround it:

• How do operating limits differ from protective limits?
• When is a sensor reading too old, implausible, or inconsistent to trust?
• Why does doubling speed quadruple ideal braking distance?
• When does redundancy help, and when can common-cause failures defeat it?
• What evidence should be required before resetting a latched stop?

The Python lab makes these ideas executable: it clips excessive speed, rejects stale and non-finite data, detects sensor disagreement, and holds a fault until deliberate recovery. Students then modify the design and defend responses for communication loss, low battery, and nearby obstacles.

Try the lab, predict the fault behavior before running it, and ask what independent evidence your own robot would need before motion is allowed again.
