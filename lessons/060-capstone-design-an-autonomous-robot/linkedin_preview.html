A capstone robot is not complete because it moves once. It is complete when its promises are measurable, its responsibilities are assigned, its failure responses are explicit, and its evidence is credible.

This Professor OS lesson guides students through:

• Translating a mission into testable requirements
• Mapping requirements to architecture and state-machine responsibilities
• Separating simulation evidence from physical validation
• Reasoning about energy reserve, sensor geometry, braking, and uncertainty
• Building a Python test harness with assertions, safe states, and timeout behavior

The RoboRover example deliberately distinguishes reference-point simulation results from frontmost physical clearance, and sample-delivery claims from actual containment measurements. That distinction is central to responsible robotics engineering.

Try the lab by changing one parameter, predicting the result before execution, and then labeling exactly what the simulation verified—and what still requires a supervised hardware test.
