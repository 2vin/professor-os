What makes a machine a robot—and does a robot have to be autonomous?

Professor OS Class 1 begins with a distinction that students will use throughout robotics:

• Robot identity concerns the physical machine, its controlled actuators, and the physical task it performs.
• Decision authority identifies whether immediate actions come from a human, a stored program, or autonomous software.
• Autonomy is task-level action selection, not independence from people.
• Feedback is a separate control concept: measured state can influence current or future action.
• A compact Python simulation lets students compare the same RoboRover hardware under three decision modes.

The lesson also introduces system boundaries, idealized motion modeling, actuator saturation, final position, and total distance traveled without pretending that a simple simulation is a physical robot.

Try the lab by changing the simulated marker distance, predicting the autonomous command sequence, and checking the resulting position. Then ask: which parts of the robot changed, and which part of the system changed only in terms of decision authority?
