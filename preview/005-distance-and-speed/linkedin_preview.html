A robot does not move because a program contains a number. It moves through a physical system whose behavior must be measured, modeled, and checked.

In Class 5, students use RoboRover to connect position with motion and learn to:

• Calculate average speed from distance and elapsed time.
• Distinguish route distance from displacement, especially after turns or reversals.
• Interpret velocity as a signed quantity in one dimension.
• Build and test a constant-velocity Python simulation.
• Compare commanded motor settings with calibrated or measured velocity.
• Investigate wheel slip, unequal motors, timing delay, and battery effects.

The lesson deliberately starts with accessible equations, then shows where the ideal model breaks down on a real robot. Students also predict simulation results, verify them with assertions, and conduct a physical timing experiment.

Try the lab with a marked track and ask one question: how closely does the measured motion match the model?
