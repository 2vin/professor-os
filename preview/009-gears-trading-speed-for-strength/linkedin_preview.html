A robot motor may spin quickly and still struggle to move a wheel up a ramp. The transmission between them changes the trade.

In Professor OS Class 9, students use RoboRover to investigate:

• How tooth counts determine a reduction ratio
• Why reducing speed can increase output torque
• How efficiency changes an ideal calculation
• Why wheel radius and traction limit real force
• How Python can compare competing gear choices

The lesson moves from a 12-tooth driver and 36-tooth driven gear to a practical climbing decision. Students calculate speed, torque, and wheel-rim force, then test their reasoning in an interactive gear-selection challenge.

Try the lab: choose a transmission for a rover that must climb while remaining fast enough for useful movement. Then ask what the simplified model leaves out—motor heating, current limits, slipping, and gearbox losses.
