How does a mobile robot turn when it has only two independently driven wheels?

This Professor OS lesson builds the answer from first principles and connects wheel-level commands to robot-level motion.

Students learn to:
• compute forward velocity from the average wheel speed;
• compute angular velocity from the wheel-speed difference and track width;
• predict straight, curved, and in-place rotational motion;
• verify the mathematics with an executable Python simulation;
• recognize why slip, wheel mismatch, and mechanical differences affect a real robot.

The lesson also prepares students for odometry by showing why measured wheel motion becomes the foundation for estimating pose.

Try the wheel-speed challenge: choose left and right speeds, predict the path, then compare your reasoning with the plotted result.
