Robots rarely move exactly as commanded. Wheel slip, calibration error, floor conditions, and battery changes all create uncertainty in motion estimates.

This Professor OS lesson introduces probability through a concrete RoboRover task: predicting how far a robot may travel toward a charging pad.

Students learn to:
• represent possible travel distances with a discrete probability distribution;
• calculate expected distance and standard deviation;
• distinguish theoretical probabilities from simulated frequencies;
• explore Monte Carlo sampling in Python; and
• recognize bias, dependence, and model mismatch in real experiments.

The key engineering idea is simple but important: an expected value is not a guarantee. A robot that stores uncertainty can decide when to slow down, gather another sensor measurement, or avoid trusting odometry alone.

Try the landing-zone simulation, then repeat it on two different surfaces and compare the resulting distributions. What changes: the center, the spread, or both?
