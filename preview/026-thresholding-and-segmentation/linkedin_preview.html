A camera image is only a measurement. Before a robot can reason about a marker, strip, or landing zone, it often needs to decide which pixels are relevant.

Professor OS Class 26 introduces thresholding and segmentation through a carefully controlled RoboRover experiment:

• Convert grayscale intensities into a binary mask.
• See how the threshold changes selected regions.
• Calculate approximate physical area from foreground pixels.
• Diagnose false positives and false negatives.
• Explore lighting, reflections, exposure, and color limitations.

The included Python simulation makes the tradeoffs visible: a threshold that rejects the background may also reject a dim target, while a lower threshold may admit distracting floor regions. Students are asked to predict outcomes before moving the slider, then verify their reasoning.

Try the lab, change the simulated scene, and ask the engineering question that matters most: what assumption makes this segmentation method work, and when will it fail?
