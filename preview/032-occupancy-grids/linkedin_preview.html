A robot does not begin with a perfect map. It builds an evidence-based approximation from sensor measurements, one small cell at a time.

This Professor OS lesson on occupancy grids guides learners through:

• the difference between free, occupied, and unknown space;
• converting world coordinates into grid indices;
• updating cells along a range-sensor beam;
• resolution, memory, pose error, and robot footprint;
• a Python simulation that compares hidden reality with the robot’s partial map.

The strongest teaching feature is the deliberate separation between the hidden environment and RoboRover’s incomplete belief about it. Students can change the robot position, sensor directions, range, and obstacles, then observe how coverage and uncertainty change.

Try the lab, remove the diagonal rays, and explain which regions remain unknown. That small experiment reveals why mapping, sensing, and localization must be designed together.
