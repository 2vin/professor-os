A robot can have an excellent map and still not know where it is on that map.

This Professor OS lesson introduces localization through RoboRover’s pose, motion estimates, sensor observations, and uncertainty. Students build understanding progressively:

• distinguish mapping from localization;
• represent position and orientation as a pose;
• calculate motion with sine and cosine;
• see how small odometry errors accumulate;
• visualize drift in a Python simulation;
• reason about how landmarks and range observations provide corrective evidence.

The lesson is careful to distinguish an odometry precursor from a complete localization system, preparing students for the next class on Bayes Rule. The graph-paper activity and prediction prompts also make the ideas testable without requiring advanced software.

Try the lab, change the distance errors, and predict the final displacement before running the code. What would your robot need to observe in order to correct its estimate?
