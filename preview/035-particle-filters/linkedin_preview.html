A robot rarely knows its exact location. It maintains uncertainty and updates that uncertainty as motion and sensor evidence arrive.

This particle-filter lesson gives students a concrete way to understand that process:

• Each particle represents a possible robot state.
• Prediction models uncertain motion.
• Sensor updates weight hypotheses by how well they explain the measurement.
• Resampling concentrates computation without pretending uncertainty has vanished.
• A Python corridor simulation makes ambiguity, particle impoverishment, effective sample size, and misleading averages visible.

The lesson also includes a human particle-filter activity and challenges students to weaken the sensor model and compare the resulting belief distribution.

Try the paper-particle simulation before running the code: can you predict when two plausible location clusters will remain? Then inspect the histogram and ask whether the tallest bar, mean, or mode is the most useful summary.
