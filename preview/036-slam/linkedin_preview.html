How can a robot build a map when its own position is uncertain?

This Professor OS lesson introduces SLAM through a carefully staged progression from odometry drift to landmark-based localization, coordinate-frame transformations, and map refinement.

Students will learn:
• Why localization and mapping depend on each other
• How to transform landmark observations into map coordinates
• Why loop closure matters—and why repeated correction is not always loop closure
• How data association and gauge freedom affect real systems
• How a deliberately simplified Python simulation exposes drift and correction

The lab is explicit about its limitations: it is a one-dimensional teaching model, not production SLAM. That transparency helps students connect the code to real filters, graph optimization, occupancy grids, and robot navigation without confusing a demonstration with a complete estimator.

Invite learners to change the odometry bias and sensing range, predict the result, then test their reasoning in the simulation.
