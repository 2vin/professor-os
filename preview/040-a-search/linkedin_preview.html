A* is often introduced as “Dijkstra plus a heuristic,” but robotics students need to understand what that heuristic changes—and what it cannot do.

This Professor OS lesson guides learners from grid-based route planning to a working Python comparison of Dijkstra and A*.

Students will learn:
• how g(n), h(n), and f(n) work together;
• why Manhattan distance is admissible for four-direction equal-cost grids;
• how weighted A* trades optimality guarantees for potentially faster search;
• why a planned path is not the same thing as a motor command;
• how maps, localization, clearance, and replanning affect a real rover.

The included lab invites students to alter the heuristic, inspect expanded nodes, and add terrain costs. Try the experiment, then ask: what assumptions must remain true for the guarantee to hold?
