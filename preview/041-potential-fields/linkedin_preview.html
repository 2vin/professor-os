Potential fields are often introduced as a simple idea: goals attract and obstacles repel. Teaching them well requires going further.

This Professor OS lesson connects the intuition to the mathematics and then to a runnable Python simulation. Students learn to:

• distinguish a scalar potential from the vector obtained from its gradient;
• model repulsion using clearance from an obstacle boundary;
• understand local minima, oscillation, footprint inflation, and command limits;
• compare endpoint collision checks with swept-segment checking;
• interpret a normalized kinematic controller rather than mistaking it for physical dynamics.

The strongest feature is the alignment between the equations, diagnostics, visual vector field, and simulation behavior. Students are asked to predict outcomes, change one parameter at a time, and measure minimum swept clearance rather than trusting a plotted path.

Try the lab with a single obstacle placed directly between the start and goal. What changes when you increase repulsion, reduce the step size, or combine the local method with a global planner?
