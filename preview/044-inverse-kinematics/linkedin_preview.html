How does a robot know which joint angles will place its tool at a desired point?

In this Professor OS lesson, students build that answer from geometry rather than treating inverse kinematics as a black box.

They learn to:
• distinguish forward kinematics from inverse kinematics;
• use the law of cosines and atan2 to solve a two-link planar arm;
• understand elbow-up and elbow-down solution branches;
• identify reachable, unreachable, and boundary targets;
• verify both solutions with forward kinematics in Python.

The lesson also connects the ideal equations to real engineering constraints: joint limits, collision risk, encoder offsets, calibration, backlash, and singular configurations. The included experiment challenges learners to predict the arm’s posture before running the simulation, then compare the prediction with the plotted result.

Try the lab with the supplied targets, then add a joint-limit filter and see how many mathematical solutions remain physically usable.
