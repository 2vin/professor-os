Reaching an object is not the same as holding it.

In Class 45, RoboRover moves from inverse kinematics to the mechanics of grasping. Students use a simple but meaningful model to connect finger force, friction, object weight, and contact geometry.

They learn to:
• distinguish normal force from the friction that supports an object;
• calculate a minimum approximate force per finger;
• see why contact placement and torque matter;
• identify practical failure modes such as slipping, crushing, misalignment, and actuator limits;
• test grasp assumptions with a Python simulation.

The key engineering idea is simple: a gripper should apply enough force to hold an object, but not so much that it damages the object or exceeds the mechanism’s limits. Just as importantly, students see why a mathematical prediction is not a guarantee on a real robot.

Try the lab by changing mass, friction coefficient, and gripper force limit. Then ask whether your robot should proceed—or refuse the lift.
