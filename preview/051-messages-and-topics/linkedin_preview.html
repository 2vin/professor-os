A robot is rarely one program. Sensors, safety logic, recorders, dashboards, and motor controllers must exchange information without becoming tightly dependent on one another.

This lesson introduces the core ROS 2 communication model through RoboRover:

• messages are structured data items;
• topics are named communication channels;
• publishers send data and subscribers consume it;
• one topic can support multiple independent subscribers;
• units, timestamps, validity, QoS, and freshness matter as much as the topic name.

Students also calculate camera-stream bandwidth, trace a distance-threshold safety decision, and run a deliberately limited Python publish/subscribe simulation. The lesson makes the important distinction between communication and physical actuation: successfully publishing a motor command does not guarantee that a wheel moved.

Try the dispatcher activity, then extend the lab with a warning subscriber or recorder node. What should your robot do when a message is missing, invalid, or too old?
