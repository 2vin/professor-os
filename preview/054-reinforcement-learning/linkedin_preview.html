How can a robot improve its behavior without being given every correct action?

Professor OS Class 54 introduces reinforcement learning through a warehouse RoboRover that compares a reliable route with a risky shortcut.

Students learn to:
• distinguish agents, environments, observations, states, rewards, and policies;
• calculate cumulative, discounted, and expected rewards;
• understand why reward design can produce undesirable behavior;
• separate a one-state bandit from sequential reinforcement learning;
• run and modify a reproducible Python experiment.

The lesson also makes an essential engineering point: reward penalties are not a substitute for emergency stops, action limits, or safety layers. The accompanying lab builds intuition for sample averages and exploration before the next class introduces Q-learning with a grid robot.

Try the lab, change the shortcut failure penalty, and see how the learned preference responds to limited evidence.
