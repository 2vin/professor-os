A mobile robot does not simply receive a destination and drive there. It repeatedly estimates where it is, chooses where to go, and converts that decision into immediate motion.

This lesson introduces students to:
• The separation between localization, planning, and control
• The difference between a long-horizon route and an immediate motion command
• Distance, heading error, angle wrapping, and proportional steering
• A Python breadth-first-search simulation with obstacles and graceful no-route handling
• Real-world limits such as wheel slip, latency, robot size, and uncertain sensing

The lab is intentionally simplified, but it makes the navigation stack visible and gives students concrete experiments to run: change obstacles, block the goal, impose a move limit, or simulate failed motion.

Try the lab, then ask: what information would the rover need before it could safely trust its planned route?
